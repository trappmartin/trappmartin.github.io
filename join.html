Prospective PhD Students

PhD positions are officially announced on the KTH jobs portal. Please check there regularly for open positions. In addition, I occasionally have potential openings that are not yet publicly listed. If you are interested in working with me, feel free to reach out by email.

What am I looking for? My group conducts fundamental research in machine learning. Successful candidates should have a strong mathematical background, strong coding skills, and a genuine interest in fundamental research in machine learning. Please check our recent publications for details on the type of research we conduct.

Further Information. PhD positions at KTH typically include 80% research and 20% teaching, and they are fully-funded (paid employment) for 4 to 5 years. For further information on the employment conditions at KTH, please see this article.

Prospective Postdocs

I regularly participate in postdoc calls and occasionally offer positions outside these calls. If you are interested in working together, please contact me by email with your CV attached and a short description of the research you aim to conduct.

Requirements. Prospective postdocs are expected to have a strong track record of publishing at relevant top-tier venues, including but not limited to NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, UAI, AISTATS, and equivalent venues in related fields (e.g., computer vision, statistics, NLP, etc.). Unfortunately, I cannot consider applications from candidates who have not previously published at top-tier venues.

Calls. I regularly participate in the following calls: Digital Futures postdocs, WASP postdocs, SSF postdocs. Feel free to suggest a different call/program if you have one in mind. Please get in touch via email before applying to a call!

Further Information. Postdoc positions at KTH are typically for 2 years with a maximum of 10% teaching load. For further information on the working conditions at KTH, please see this article.

Prospective MSc Thesis Students

I supervise KTH students interested in carrying out an academic Master’s thesis on machine learning, i.e., fundamental research in machine learning and AI. Only in exceptional cases, I supervise industrial MSc theses. I do not supervise external students.

Topics. Potential topics include: quantifying uncertainties and model complexity in deep learning, tractable probabilistic reasoning with probabilistic circuits, analyzing sensitivities in deep learning arising from model compression. Feel free to propose you own thesis topic.

How to. If the above matches your profile, and you are interested in working with me on machine learning research topics, please get in touch by email with your CV attached and a short description of the thesis topic.
